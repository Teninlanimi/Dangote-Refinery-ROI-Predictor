How the estimate works

The model values one share from the refinery's profit and a price-to-earnings multiple, adds dividends, and compares the result with the ₦525 offer price, in naira and in US dollars.

The price being tested

The Dangote Petroleum Refinery public offer is 4.1bn shares at ₦525 (SEC-approved, open 14 September to 27 October 2026, NGX listing expected November or December 2026), out of 120.13bn shares in total. That implies a market value of about ₦63.1tn, or roughly $48bn at the ₦1,312.5 per dollar the prospectus itself uses.

The refinery only became profitable in H1 2026: $1.8bn net income on $14.4bn revenue, a 12.5% net margin, after losses of $1.51bn in 2024 and $476m in 2025. Annualised, that is about $3.6bn of profit a year, so the offer price is about 13.4 times annualised profit, almost exactly Dangote Cement's 13.5 times.

The calculation

  • Value per share = net income × P/E ÷ 120.13bn shares.
  • Growth: profit compounds at the scenario rate, reflecting the ramp-up toward 1.4m barrels a day by 2029 and petrochemicals.
  • Dividends: from year 2, a share of profit is paid out. Dividends in dollars have been promised but not yet formally approved.
  • Naira: the exit value is converted at a depreciating exchange rate (8% a year by default), so the return is shown in naira and in US dollars.

How today's market feeds in

When "Adjust for today's market" is on, three live inputs change the scenarios:

  • Brent crude → revenue. Revenue is scaled by the latest Brent price divided by its H1 2026 average, because product prices follow crude.
  • Crack spread → net margin. A refinery earns the gap between what it sells products for and what it pays for crude. The model tracks the 3-2-1 crack spread: two barrels of petrol plus one of diesel, minus three barrels of crude, divided by three. Gross profit ($5.2bn a year in H1 2026) moves with the spread; the other $1.6bn of costs stay fixed. Dearer crude with an unchanged spread therefore lowers the margin and leaves profit roughly flat.
  • Exchange rate → exit value. The future exchange rate starts from today's rate instead of the offer-date rate. Your ₦525 entry cost doesn't change.

Each live adjustment is shown in words under the result, and the data pipeline page shows where every number came from.

What the model leaves out

  • The greenshoe (up to 30% more shares) and dilution when the private placement lock-up ends.
  • Taxes, crude-supply arrangements with NNPC, and the petrochemicals business as a separate line.
  • US New York Harbor prices stand in for the West African market, and the 3-2-1 mix is not Dangote's exact product slate.
  • Government pressure on pump prices, which affects how much of the spread the refinery keeps.
  • The free exchange-rate feed is a market mid-rate, not the official or parallel rate.

This is an educational scenario model, not investment advice. An offer price is not a fair value, and shares can lose money. Read the prospectus and do your own research before you invest.
